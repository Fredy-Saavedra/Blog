FamilyAt home

The people who feel like home

Family: the people who feel like home

My family is not perfect, but we show up for one another. A shared meal, a patient conversation, or a funny old story can turn an ordinary day into a good memory. They remind me that listening is one way of saying, “I care.”

My takeaway: Home is less a place than a feeling we create together.
HobbiesSlow moments

A little room for curiosity

Hobbies and interests: making room for curiosity

In my free time, I enjoy music, reading, and learning things just because they interest me. A hobby gives my mind a break from routine. It also teaches me to be a beginner, practise patiently, and enjoy progress instead of waiting to be perfect.

My takeaway: Small interests can make everyday life feel bigger.
WorkWorking together

More than a to-do list

Work: more than a to-do list

Work is not only about finishing tasks; it is also about communicating clearly and treating people with respect. When I organise my time, ask questions, and support my teammates, challenges become easier to manage. I am learning that being reliable matters as much as being talented.

My takeaway: Good work includes both effort and empathy.
TravelOn the road

The best souvenir is a story

Travel: the best souvenir is a story

When I travel, I want to notice the details: the local food, the sounds of a new street, and the way people welcome visitors. A trip can change the questions we ask about our own routines. I do not need to go far to be surprised; I only need to pay attention.

My takeaway: Travel begins with an open mind.
Current eventsStaying informed

News with a human face

Current events: news has a human face

News can feel overwhelming, so I try to look beyond the headline. I ask who is affected, what context is missing, and whether a source provides evidence. Staying informed should not mean sharing every alarming story. It can mean choosing reliable information and making space for thoughtful conversation.

My takeaway: Being informed is a habit, not a race.
Critical thinking & media literacyPause before sharing

Think twice, click once

Critical thinking and media literacy: think twice before sharing

A post can look convincing and still leave out important facts. Before I believe or share a claim, I check who created it, when it was published, and what evidence supports it. I compare it with other trustworthy sources and look for the original context. Images and statistics need the same care: they can be edited, old, or used to tell a different story. Critical thinking does not mean distrusting everything; it means asking fair questions and changing my mind when better evidence appears.

Questions I ask: Who benefits from this message? What is missing? Can I verify it somewhere else?
Crime & punishmentJustice and humanity

Justice should leave room for change

Crime and punishment: justice should leave room for change

Crime can cause deep harm, and people who are hurt deserve support and justice. At the same time, punishment alone may not prevent future harm. A fair response should protect the community, respect due process, and consider the needs of victims. Education, rehabilitation, and opportunities to rebuild a life can also make society safer. These ideas do not erase responsibility; they ask what response can repair harm and reduce the chance of it happening again.

My question: How can a justice system hold people accountable while making room for rehabilitation?

One step at a time. Writing these posts helped me practise English and connect everyday experiences with bigger ideas. I want to keep asking questions, listening carefully, and learning from different perspectives.
